The second pass reverses the order to help balance gradual changes across days. Runs need not be on consecutive days; choose similar routines.

Aim for a day and a night. For example, start at 9 a.m. and finish at 7 a.m. the next morning, leaving time to recharge.

Use a duration your battery can comfortably support. Only uninterrupted runs of at least 12 hours enter the comparison. Start at a similar charge level and time of day each time.

02

Prepare once.

  • Finish updates first. Confirm Binary Heart History has Settings > Battery test. Use Start recording in History settings to grant heart-rate and background access, and check that readings arrive. Keep the app and OS versions fixed for the series.
  • Keep the face consistent. Use Binary Pulse throughout, with the same ordinary complication count and providers, brightness, always-on display, colors, and decimal backdrop. Keep graph contrast, time window, and labels fixed throughout the series.
  • Keep use comparable. Keep connectivity settings consistent. Pick days with similar sleep, activity, phone proximity, and screen use. Note unusual workouts, travel, poor signal, or heavy use so we can interpret the results.

Pausing recording leaves a gap in heart-rate history. Readings from that gap cannot be recovered later.

03

For every run.

  1. Set up the next mode.

    Binary Heart History > Settings > Battery testChoose the next mode in the schedule, then tap Set up mode. The app applies the recording setting for you.

  2. Match the face layout.

    Long-press Binary Pulse, open its editor, and change Layout. For Baseline and Tracking only, choose a layout without heart history. For Tracking + graph, choose one with heart history. Keep the same ordinary complication count.

    For a graph run, check that the graph is visible and populated with recorded readings. The background uses the fixed Heart history graph provider. If the app asks you to wait for readings, wait before starting.

  3. Unplug, then start.

    Return to Battery test and tick Face layout matches this mode. Take the watch off its charger, tap Start run, and confirm the selected mode says it is running. Close the app.

  4. Wear it normally.

    Keep the watch unplugged and leave the face and settings alone. Avoid app updates, reboots, and force-stopping History during the run. Skip the optional Add battery reading for this series; the start and finish readings are enough for the comparison.

    Keep a short note elsewhere of anything unusual. Frequent battery checks wake the screen and add their own drain.

  5. Finish before charging.

    At the planned end, return to Battery test and tap Finish run while still unplugged. If you need to recharge early, finish first; a run under 12 hours will be saved but left out of the comparison.

    Yes, save if the run stayed unplugged with the same face and settings, without an update, reboot, or force-stop.

    No, exclude if there was an interruption, even a brief charge. Repeat that mode on another comparable day. Brief interruptions may escape automatic detection.

  6. Check the save, then recharge.

    Open Results to confirm the run is saved and see whether it was excluded. The app restores your previous recording mode; it cannot restore the face's Layout. Change the layout back if needed, then recharge for the next run.

    If the app shows Retry restore, complete that before starting the next setup. Keep the time window and labels fixed throughout the series so the results stay in one comparison group.

04

Read the difference.

After two eligible runs per mode, open Battery test > Results. Lower pp/h means less drain: it is battery percentage points per hour.

Example only, not a measured result

An extra 0.20 pp/h projects to about 4.8 extra battery percentage points over 24 hours, if the same drain rate holds.

This is a practical estimate of whole-watch drain. Daily variation and the coarse battery gauge limit how precisely we can attribute small differences.

Keep the results.

Use Share results if the watch offers a compatible receiving app. It exports the battery observations for later analysis and plotting, with no heart-rate readings. If sharing is unavailable, use the charts on the watch and copy down each mode's rate plus your notes.

Save or export when the series is complete. Retention is limited; the app shows its limits at the bottom of Results.

For Binary Pulse and Binary Heart History. Run the experiment on your actual watch; emulator battery readings do not measure real battery impact.

This guide works offline. The diagnostic adds no background battery polling and keeps results on the watch until you choose to share them.
